What a façade clean really involves

Before any equipment is chosen, these practical questions decide whether — and how — a façade can be cleaned well.

  • Height and façade access

    Building height, setbacks, overhangs and surrounding structures shape what can reach each elevation.

  • Surface and material

    Glass, cladding, stone and coatings each respond differently to water, pressure and cleaning agents.

  • Cleaning requirement

    Routine soiling, hard-water staining or construction residue call for different methods and frequency.

  • Water supply and management

    Source, quality and volume of water, and where run-off goes during and after the work.

  • Occupied-building operations

    Tenants, residents, visitors and trading hours that the work has to plan around.

  • Site access and coordination

    Ground-level work zones, vehicle access, security interfaces and other contractors on site.

  • Risk and permissions

    Site-specific risk assessment and the approvals that apply to the location and activity.

  • Not every building suits drone cleaning

    Some façades are better served by rope access, cradles or mechanised methods. We’ll tell you which.

The DroneX approach

The right solution depends on the building, the façade material, the operating environment and what the clean needs to achieve.

Our facility and property management background means we look at the façade as part of a working building — with MEP services, drainage, security routines and occupants who all need to be considered. Drone capability is then applied where it suits that picture.

  • Building knowledge informs the method, not just the flight plan.
  • Cleaning methodology is selected for the surface and the soiling.
  • Operational planning keeps the building running while work is under way.

Where drone façade cleaning may be suitable

Typical candidates, each confirmed through a site assessment.

Commercial glass façades

Curtain-wall office buildings where regular, consistent cleaning supports the building’s image.

High-rise and multi-storey exteriors

Elevations where conventional access is slow, disruptive or difficult to arrange.

Commercial and mixed-use properties

Selected properties where the work can be coordinated around tenants and public areas.

Other exteriors, after assessment

Further surfaces considered case by case, once compatibility and access are confirmed.

Suitability always depends on the actual site, the equipment available, surface condition, applicable permissions and the scope agreed with you. We will recommend an alternative method where drone cleaning is not the right fit.

Cleaning methodology

Effective façade cleaning needs more than a flying platform.

Each of these factors is evaluated for your building and cleaning objective before a method is agreed. Specific products, settings and equipment are confirmed in the proposal for that site.

Method assessmentConfirmed per site

  1. Surface compatibility

    How the façade material and any coatings tolerate water, agents and pressure.

  2. Cleaning agents and dilution

    Whether an agent is needed, which one, and at what concentration.

  3. Water quality

    The water source and whether treatment is needed for a streak-free finish.

  4. Flow rate and pressure

    Enough to clean effectively without risk to seals, joints or finishes.

  5. Nozzle selection

    Spray pattern and reach matched to the surface and distance.

  6. Rinsing requirements

    How residues are removed and the finish left clean.

  7. Wastewater and environment

    Run-off, drainage and environmental considerations, where applicable.

From enquiry to report

The exact process and approvals depend on the site and the project requirements.

  1. Initial enquiry

    You share the property, its location and what you need. We confirm whether a site assessment is the right next step.

  2. Site and façade assessment

    We review façade materials, access, water supply, drainage, surroundings and how the building operates.

  3. Solution engineering and scope definition

    We recommend the method — drone or otherwise — and define a clear scope, programme and exclusions.

  4. Risk assessment, planning and authorisations

    Method statement, work zones, emergency arrangements and the permissions that apply are put in place.

  5. Execution, quality verification and reporting

    Work is delivered with the building team, quality is checked and completion is documented with any observations.

Safety and coordination

Façade work happens above people, property and public space. It is planned that way.

  • Site-specific risk assessment and a written method statement.
  • Work-zone planning at ground level, with exclusion areas where needed.
  • Weather considerations, including wind and rain limits for the planned method.
  • Emergency arrangements agreed with building management before work starts.
  • Relevant approvals for the location and activity, as applicable.
  • Stakeholder coordination with property managers, security, engineering and occupants.
