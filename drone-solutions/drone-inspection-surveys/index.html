See the parts of your building you can’t easily reach.

Planned drone visual surveys of façades, roofs and difficult-to-access areas — scoped around what you need to know and documented for your maintenance team.

Request a Site Assessment What a visual survey can’t do

Know the condition. Then plan the work.

High-level areas of a building are often inspected late, because getting someone close to them takes scaffolding, cradles or rope access.

A drone visual survey can give your property or engineering team a closer look at those areas first — so decisions about maintenance, cleaning or further investigation are made with better information. The survey is planned as a building-services task: what are we looking for, who needs the findings, and how will the work fit around the building’s operations?

What a survey can cover

The exact areas and level of detail are agreed during scoping, subject to site conditions, equipment and permissions.

  • Façades and elevations

    Visible condition of glazing, cladding, sealant lines, fixings and staining across elevations.

  • Roofs and roof plant areas

    Roof coverings, drainage outlets, parapets and the visible condition around rooftop equipment.

  • Hard-to-access areas

    Canopies, atria, high-level signage and other areas that are difficult to view from the ground.

  • Pre-cleaning surveys

    Façade condition before a cleaning programme, to inform method selection and protect sensitive surfaces.

  • Post-work verification

    A visual record after cleaning or maintenance to support quality review against the agreed scope.

  • Your question comes first

    We scope each survey around what you need to find out, not around how much we can fly.

What you receive

Documentation is agreed in the scope, so you get information your team can act on.

Formats depend on the survey purpose and the equipment used for your site. We confirm them in the proposal.

Documentation optionsAgreed per scope

  1. Visual record

    Photographs and, where agreed, video of the surveyed areas.

  2. Located observations

    Visible issues noted against elevation, level or roof area so they can be found again.

  3. Summary report

    A plain-language summary of what was observed and what was not accessible.

  4. Recommended next steps

    Where further investigation, cleaning or maintenance may be needed.

What a visual survey is — and isn’t.

Being clear about limits is part of a professional survey.

  • It records what is visible

    A visual survey observes surfaces. It does not test materials, measure hidden defects or open up the building fabric.

  • It is not a structural assessment

    Where observations suggest a structural or safety concern, a qualified engineer should assess it.

  • Conditions affect results

    Weather, light, reflections and access to flying areas can limit what is captured on the day.

  • Permissions apply

    Every survey depends on the approvals that apply to the location and on a site-specific risk assessment.

How a survey is delivered

The exact process and approvals depend on the site and what the survey needs to show.

  1. Define the question

    What you need to know, which areas matter, and who will use the findings.

  2. Site assessment

    Access, surroundings, occupancy, flying constraints and the permissions that apply.

  3. Scope and plan

    Survey areas, documentation formats, timing and a site-specific risk assessment.

  4. Coordinated survey

    Carried out with your property, security and engineering teams, with work zones in place.

  5. Report and review

    Documented observations, accessibility notes and recommended next steps.
